The Problem

Power Fails When
Lives Depend on It

High-altitude climbing has over 50 million active participants worldwide, growing 3–5% annually. Yet every existing power solution fails in exactly the conditions climbers face.

🔋

Lithium Batteries Collapse in Cold

At −20 °C, most devices retain less than 30% of their rated capacity. Sudden shutdown of GPS or satellite communicators has contributed to fatalities in documented mountain disasters.

☁️

Solar Panels Are Useless in Storms

High-altitude climbers routinely encounter blizzards, overcast skies, and polar nights — exactly when emergency power is most critical and solar panels produce nothing.

⚖️

Pro Equipment Is Too Heavy

High-capacity portable power stations and fuel cells can provide stable power, but their bulk and weight are incompatible with the ultralight philosophy of alpine climbing.

⚡

No "Generate As You Go" Option Exists

All current solutions are "store-then-drain" models. Once depleted, they cannot be replenished in the field. A passive, on-demand generation solution has not existed — until now.

Working Principle

Boiling Water &
Falling Snow = Electricity

Melting snow for drinking water is an unavoidable part of high-altitude climbing. We turned that necessary step into a continuous power source.

1

Place the Lid on Your Pot

The SnowPower lid fits standard mountaineering cookware. Set it on your pot just as you would a normal lid — no extra setup required.

2

Load Snow on Top

Pack snow onto the lid's upper surface. The boiling water inside creates a hot side; the snow above creates a cold side. The temperature gap exceeds 100 °C.

3

The Seebeck Effect Generates Power

SnTe thermoelectric modules sandwiched inside the lid convert the temperature gradient directly into electricity via the Seebeck Effect — with no moving parts.

4

Charge Your Devices via USB

A 5S2P thermoelectric array produces ~25 V raw, stepped down to a stable 5 V / 7.5 W via an onboard converter, delivered through a standard USB-C port.

The Science

Built on a
Breakthrough Material

The core of SnowPower is a novel SnTe (Tin Telluride) thermoelectric material, optimised through vacancy-based high-entropy engineering — outperforming traditional materials without any toxic elements.

Seebeck Effect

When two sides of a semiconductor are held at different temperatures, a voltage is generated. The larger the temperature difference and the higher the material's Seebeck coefficient, the more power is produced.

SnTe — Eco-Friendly & Efficient

Unlike the industry-standard PbTe (which contains toxic lead), SnTe is non-toxic and environmentally safe. Its band structure mirrors PbTe but operates cleanly across the 300–773 K range critical for our application.

Entropy Engineering & Vacancy Strategy

By introducing atomic vacancies via a "cocktail effect" strategy, we simultaneously increased the Seebeck coefficient (via band convergence) and suppressed thermal conductivity (via multi-scale phonon scattering).

Dual Patent Protection

The In-Sb doping process and the "cookware-TEG integrated structure" are protected by two pending invention patents covering material composition, preparation process, and product architecture.

Thermoelectric Figure of Merit (ZT) — SnTe Performance

Room Temperature ZT≈ 0.4
Average ZT (300–773 K)≈ 1.0
Peak ZT (623 K)≈ 1.3

Higher ZT = more electricity from the same temperature difference. Average ZT > 1.0 is a benchmark for high-performance thermoelectrics.
